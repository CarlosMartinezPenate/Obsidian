# Working title

**Molecular Organization and Remodeling of Photosynthetic Antenna
Systems**

> **Title status:** Working title. Final title = **N/A**.

# Abstract

Photosynthetic organisms dynamically organize and remodel
light-harvesting antenna systems to balance efficient photon capture
with changing environmental and metabolic demands. Despite major
advances in structural biology, the molecular transitions that connect
antenna architecture to acclimation, remodeling, and degradation remain
incompletely resolved. This proposal addresses these transitions in two
evolutionarily distinct photosynthetic antenna systems: the
membrane-associated peridinin--chlorophyll *a/c*-binding complexes
(acpPCs) of Symbiodiniaceae dinoflagellates and the phycobilisome (PBS)
degradation machinery of the cyanobacterium *Acaryochloris marina*
MBIC11017.

The dinoflagellate arm will use five cultured Symbiodiniaceae strains
provided by Tingting Xiang (University of California, Riverside):
*Symbiodinium linucheae* SSA01, *S. necroappetens* SSA02, *S. pilosum*
SSA03, *Breviolum minutum* SSB01, and *Effrenium voratum* SSE01. Their
axenic status was reported at source for the originating strain
collection but has not been independently verified in the present
cultures. Preliminary work has established maintenance and scale-up of
all five strains and whole-culture absorption/fluorescence measurements.
The proposed work will first establish a comparative biochemical
workflow for isolation and characterization of membrane antenna
fractions, with emphasis on acpPC-containing complexes. It will then
determine whether chronic irradiance acclimation produces reproducible
changes in acpPC biochemical organization, pigment environment, and
excitation-energy-transfer behavior. The number and intensity of
irradiance treatments will be established empirically and are currently
**N/A**.

The cyanobacterial arm will investigate how NblA- and NblB-dependent
processes remodel native *A. marina* PBS during degradation. Published
work establishes NblA as a PBS-binding degradation factor and
proteolytic adaptor, while NblB is implicated in PBP/bilin remodeling
and displays NblA-dependent activity in cell-free degradation assays
[@baier2014; @levi2018; @hu2020]. However, NblA and NblB were not
detectably interacting directly in one biochemical study, leaving
unresolved how NblA-dependent PBS remodeling is coupled to NblB action
[@hu2020]. Preliminary Harris-laboratory work provides a tractable
native system: purified AmPBS, AmNblA, and AmNblB are available;
previous work in the laboratory has also established differential
NblA-dependent PBS-disassembly phenotypes. The proposed work will define
AmNblA-dependent molecular states of AmPBS and test how these states
alter AmNblB activity. Structural analysis of AmNblB will be integrated
with mass photometry, fluorescence spectroscopy, protein biochemistry,
and targeted mutagenesis to identify molecular determinants of substrate
recognition and bilin/PBP remodeling.

Together, these projects test a common thesis-level concept:
photosynthetic antenna function depends not only on static architecture
but on regulated transitions between molecular states. By comparing
acclimatory remodeling of a membrane antenna with regulated disassembly
of a soluble extrinsic antenna, this work aims to reveal general
principles by which pigment--protein complexes reorganize while
preserving or redirecting excitation-energy flow.

# 1. Scientific background and rationale

## 1.1 Photosynthetic antennae as dynamic molecular systems

Photosynthetic antenna systems extend the spectral and spatial
cross-section for photon capture and direct excitation energy toward
reaction centers. Their performance depends on the organization of
pigments within protein scaffolds, the association of antenna proteins
into higher-order complexes, and the energetic coupling among
chromophores. Antenna architecture is therefore not merely structural:
changes in protein composition, oligomerization, pigment occupancy,
chromophore geometry, or interaction with reaction centers can alter
excitation-energy transfer, quenching, and photoprotection.

The central premise of this proposal is that antennae should be studied
as **dynamic molecular systems**. Two forms of remodeling are
particularly informative. First, long-term acclimation to irradiance can
alter antenna abundance and composition while photosynthetic cells
remain viable and functional. Second, nutrient or environmental stress
can trigger controlled disassembly and recycling of antenna material.
These processes occur in very different organisms and protein systems,
but both require coordinated transitions between defined
pigment--protein states.

This proposal therefore investigates two complementary systems:

1.  **Symbiodiniaceae acpPC-containing membrane antennae**, in which
    irradiance-dependent acclimation provides a route to study adaptive
    remodeling of pigment--protein organization.
2.  ***Acaryochloris marina* phycobilisomes**, in which
    NblA/NblB-dependent degradation provides a route to study controlled
    antenna disassembly and pigment remodeling.

The proposal does **not** assume that these systems share a homologous
molecular mechanism. Their connection is conceptual: both provide
experimentally accessible systems in which antenna organization can be
linked to regulated molecular remodeling.

## 1.2 Symbiodiniaceae light harvesting

Symbiodiniaceae possess unusual photosynthetic antenna systems adapted
to marine light environments. Their major light-harvesting pigments
include chlorophyll *a*, chlorophyll *c*$_2$, and the carotenoid
peridinin. Peridinin-containing antennae occur in at least two major
biochemical contexts: soluble peridinin--chlorophyll *a* protein (PCP)
and membrane-associated chlorophyll *a/c*$_2$--peridinin protein
complexes (acpPC). PCP has been extensively characterized
spectroscopically and structurally, whereas membrane acpPC complexes are
less completely understood in native and acclimation-dependent states
[@jiang2012a; @carbonera2014; @jovine1995].

Recent cryo-EM work has substantially advanced structural understanding
of membrane antenna organization in dinoflagellates. A 2.8 Å structure
of a *Symbiodinium* PSI--LHCI supercomplex resolved 13 AcpPC proteins
surrounding the PSI core, providing direct structural information on
pigment organization and predicted energy-transfer routes [@zhao2024].
Earlier biochemical work also demonstrated membrane-bound
light-harvesting complexes in dinoflagellates and provided precedents
for detergent-based isolation [@jovine1995]. These findings establish
that acpPC architecture can now be interrogated in a mechanistically
meaningful structural context.

Light acclimation provides an experimentally relevant perturbation of
this system. Classical work showed that Symbiodiniaceae alter
chlorophyll--protein complexes in response to photon flux and that these
responses can be species dependent [@iglesias-prieto]. High-light
studies further indicate that membrane antenna components, including
acpPC-associated excitation, can undergo strong non-photochemical
quenching rather than simply disconnecting soluble PCP from downstream
antennae [@kanazawa2014]. Thus, irradiance affects antenna function, but
the molecular relationship between chronic acclimation and the
biochemical state of native acpPC complexes remains incompletely
resolved.

### Knowledge gap 1

> **How does chronic irradiance acclimation alter the biochemical
> organization of membrane-bound acpPC complexes across phylogenetically
> distinct Symbiodiniaceae, and how are those changes related to
> chromophore environment and excitation-energy-transfer behavior?**

The proposal deliberately does not assume that acclimation must change
acpPC oligomerization, pigment stoichiometry, subunit composition, or
chromophore geometry. These are candidate mechanisms to be tested.

## 1.3 The *Acaryochloris marina* phycobilisome

Phycobilisomes are large bilin-containing antenna complexes found in
cyanobacteria and red algae [@adir2020]. Their modular architecture
allows substantial remodeling in response to nutrient availability and
environmental conditions. In *A. marina* MBIC11017, the PBS system is
particularly informative because this chlorophyll-*d*-dominated
cyanobacterium contains a horizontally acquired phycocyanin-based
light-harvesting system integrated into the cellular regulatory network.
Recent work has shown that the PC/PBS system contributes access to
spectral regions not efficiently captured by chlorophyll *d* and has
become integrated into ancestral regulatory responses.

The AmPBS is a compact rod-like antenna assembled predominantly from
phycocyanin and associated linker proteins. Previous structural and
spectroscopic studies have shown that AmPBS/AmPC has unusual red-shifted
fluorescence and multiple PC isoforms, allowing efficient energy
transfer in the chlorophyll-*d* photosynthetic context. These features
make AmPBS a well-characterized native substrate for studying regulated
antenna disassembly.

## 1.4 NblA, NblB, and controlled PBS degradation

During nitrogen starvation and other stresses, cyanobacteria degrade
PBSs and recycle their nitrogen-rich phycobiliproteins. NblA is a small
protein central to this process. Structural, biochemical, and genetic
work established that NblA binds phycobiliproteins and can act as an
adaptor connecting PBS substrates to ATP-dependent Clp proteolysis
[@baier2014]. NblA proteins can also exhibit substrate specificity,
suggesting that PBS degradation is not necessarily a nonspecific
collapse of the antenna.

NblB is a homolog of bilin-lyase-related proteins and is also required
for normal PBS degradation. Work by Levi and colleagues implicated NblB
in degradation of both PC and APC and provided cell-free evidence for
involvement in pigment removal, with NblB activity dependent on NblA
[@levi2018]. However, Hu and colleagues subsequently showed that the
reaction network is more complex: NblA and NblB can both influence
PBS/PBP dissociation, interact with apo- and holo-PBPs and bilin lyases,
and modulate chromophore attachment, detachment, and transfer.
Importantly, that study did not detect direct NblA--NblB interaction
[@hu2020].

Additional degradation factors, including NblD, demonstrate that PBS
degradation is a multi-component process rather than a simple linear
NblA→NblB→protease pathway [@krauspe2021; @krauspe2022]. The present
proposal therefore focuses specifically on the **NblA/NblB/PBS
mechanistic module** without claiming that it constitutes the complete
cellular degradation pathway.

### Knowledge gap 2

> **How is NblA-dependent remodeling of a native PBS mechanistically
> coupled to NblB activity on chromophorylated phycobiliproteins?**

Three aspects are particularly unresolved:

-   the molecular state of the PBS/PBP substrate recognized by NblB;
-   the structural determinants by which NblB recognizes and remodels
    that substrate;
-   why NblB activity can depend on NblA despite the absence of evidence
    for a stable NblA--NblB binary complex.

# 2. Overall research objective

The overall objective is to determine how photosynthetic antenna
complexes reorganize in response to physiological demands by resolving
molecular transitions in two complementary pigment--protein systems.

The proposal addresses four linked objectives:

1.  **Establish and compare native membrane antenna preparations from
    five Symbiodiniaceae strains and define acpPC-containing biochemical
    phenotypes.**
2.  **Determine how chronic irradiance acclimation remodels acpPC
    organization and associated photophysical behavior.**
3.  **Determine how AmNblA remodels native AmPBS and whether it
    generates reproducible degradation-associated molecular states.**
4.  **Determine how AmNblB recognizes and remodels AmPBS-derived
    substrates and establish the structural basis of its activity.**

# 3. Central hypotheses

## 3.1 Dinoflagellate hypothesis

> **Chronic irradiance acclimation induces reproducible changes in the
> biochemical organization of membrane-bound acpPC complexes, modifying
> the energetic environment and organization of their bound chromophores
> and thereby altering spectral properties and
> excitation-energy-transfer behavior.**

This hypothesis does not specify a priori whether the dominant
remodeling variable is subunit composition, pigment occupancy,
oligomerization, association with photosystems, or chromophore geometry.

## 3.2 Cyanobacterial hypothesis

> **NblA and NblB coordinate PBS degradation primarily through changes
> in the molecular state of the phycobiliprotein substrate rather than
> through a stable NblA--NblB binary complex. AmNblA-dependent
> remodeling of AmPBS generates or enriches substrate states with
> altered susceptibility to AmNblB, while the lyase-related architecture
> of AmNblB enables recognition and remodeling of chromophorylated
> PBPs.**

This is a working mechanistic hypothesis, not an established model.
Competing models are retained explicitly:

-   **Sequential substrate remodeling:** AmNblA generates a state
    subsequently preferred by AmNblB.
-   **Simultaneous/substrate-mediated cooperation:** NblA and NblB act
    together through a transient PBP-associated assembly.
-   **Parallel activity:** NblA and NblB remodel different properties of
    the PBS with limited direct mechanistic dependence in the purified
    system.
-   **Extended system:** full bilin remodeling requires additional
    components such as bilin lyases and/or other degradation factors.

# 5. Research plan

# Chapter I --- Comparative biochemical organization of Symbiodiniaceae membrane antennae

## Aim 1. Establish a comparative workflow for isolation and characterization of acpPC-containing membrane antenna complexes

### Rationale

Recent high-resolution structures demonstrate that AcpPC proteins form
extensive membrane-associated antenna systems around dinoflagellate
photosystems [@zhao2024]. However, comparative biochemical
characterization across the five available Symbiodiniaceae strains is
lacking. Before irradiance-dependent remodeling can be interpreted, it
is necessary to establish what membrane antenna material can be
reproducibly isolated from each strain under a common reference
condition.

### Experimental strategy

Cells from each strain will be harvested under defined reference growth
conditions. A membrane-isolation workflow will be optimized to enrich
thylakoid/membrane material while preserving pigment--protein complexes.
Membranes will subsequently be solubilized using mild detergent
conditions informed by published dinoflagellate membrane-complex
isolation and PSI--antenna purification studies
[@jovine1995; @kato2020; @zhao2024].

The initial workflow will prioritize biochemical tractability rather
than maximal purity:

1.  cell harvest and disruption;
2.  membrane/thylakoid enrichment;
3.  mild detergent solubilization;
4.  separation of major pigment--protein populations;
5.  identification of acpPC-containing fractions using complementary
    biochemical and spectroscopic evidence.

Sucrose-density separation, native electrophoresis, SDS-PAGE,
absorption/fluorescence spectroscopy, HPLC pigment analysis, proteomics,
and ÄKTA/FPLC are candidate tools. Their exact order and conditions will
be determined empirically.

### Identification criteria

A fraction will not be designated "acpPC" solely on the basis of color
or fluorescence. Identification should combine, where feasible:

-   protein composition;
-   pigment composition;
-   spectral properties;
-   apparent molecular/oligomeric state;
-   comparison with published acpPC/PSI--AcpPC information.

### Expected outcome

The primary outcome will be a reproducible biochemical map of membrane
pigment--protein fractions for each strain and identification of
fractions suitable for comparative acpPC analysis.

### Alternative outcomes

If intact acpPC complexes cannot be maintained during isolation, the
project will distinguish failure of extraction from genuine biological
absence. Detergent identity/concentration, ionic strength, membrane
preparation, and separation method will be optimized. If
photosystem-bound and free antenna populations cannot initially be
separated cleanly, the first comparative unit may be an acpPC-enriched
membrane fraction rather than a purified molecular species.

### Decision criterion for Chapter II

A strain/condition will advance to mechanistic analysis when a
reproducible acpPC-containing preparation can be generated and compared
using at least two orthogonal readouts.

# Chapter II --- Irradiance-dependent remodeling of acpPC

## Aim 2. Determine how chronic irradiance acclimation changes acpPC biochemical and photophysical states

### Rationale

Symbiodiniaceae acclimate to changes in photon flux, and classic work
indicates species-specific changes in chlorophyll--protein complexes and
pigment composition [@iglesias-prieto]. High-light experiments also
implicate membrane antennae, including acpPC, in photoprotective
quenching [@kanazawa2014]. What remains unclear is whether chronic
acclimation produces reproducible biochemical remodeling of native acpPC
complexes and whether such remodeling differs among Symbiodiniaceae
lineages.

### Experimental design

The five strains will first be screened under a controlled irradiance
series. The **number of irradiance conditions, exact photon flux
densities, duration of acclimation, and operational criterion for stable
acclimation are N/A** and will be established by preliminary
growth/light-response experiments.

The screen will measure culture-level phenotypes using:

-   growth/OD$_{750}$;
-   flow cytometric scatter and red autofluorescence;
-   microscopy where informative;
-   whole-culture absorption and fluorescence spectroscopy.

Flow cytometry is intended as a high-throughput phenotypic readout
rather than direct evidence of acpPC molecular state.

After acclimation, membrane antenna preparations will be generated using
the Aim 1 workflow. Comparative analysis will test candidate remodeling
variables including:

-   acpPC abundance;
-   subunit composition;
-   pigment composition/occupancy;
-   oligomeric or higher-order state;
-   association with larger photosynthetic complexes;
-   absorption/fluorescence properties;
-   excitation-energy-transfer behavior.

The initial five-strain screen is not intended to support equally deep
structural analysis of all strain × irradiance combinations. Two or
three informative contrasts will be selected for mechanistic follow-up
based on reproducibility and magnitude/type of molecular phenotype.

**Working mechanistic model**

$$
\text{irradiance acclimation} \rightarrow \text{acpPC biochemical remodeling} \rightarrow \text{altered chromophore environment/organization} \rightarrow \text{altered spectral and EET behavior}
$$

Every arrow is testable; none is assumed.

### Mechanistic follow-up

Selected contrasts will be investigated using available institutional
capabilities including proteomics, pigment HPLC, chromatography,
fluorescence spectroscopy, and structural approaches where justified.
The exact method for resolving chromophore geometry and the exact
time-resolved EET method are currently **N/A**.

### Falsification and alternative interpretation

The central acpPC-remodeling hypothesis would be weakened if stable
irradiance acclimation produces reproducible whole-cell physiological
changes but no reproducible difference in acpPC biochemical composition,
organization, or photophysical behavior under validated isolation
conditions.

Such a result would remain informative: acclimation may be dominated by
antenna abundance, reaction-center regulation, xanthophyll-cycle
activity, or other cellular processes rather than intrinsic acpPC
remodeling.

# Chapter III --- Generation of degradation-associated AmPBS states by AmNblA

## Aim 3. Determine whether AmNblA generates reproducible molecular states of native AmPBS

### Rationale

NblA is established as a central PBS-degradation factor and can bind PBP
substrates and recruit Clp machinery [@baier2014]. Preliminary
Harris-laboratory results further support differential
substrate/disassembly behavior among NblA proteins. However, the
molecular state generated by AmNblA on native AmPBS is not defined.

This chapter will therefore avoid presupposing that AmNblA produces a
specific "partially disassembled" intermediate. Instead, an
AmNblA-remodeled state, provisionally denoted AmPBS\*, will be defined
operationally from experimental evidence.

### Operational definition of AmPBS\*

**AmPBS\* identity = N/A.**

For the purposes of hypothesis testing, AmPBS\* will be defined as a
**reproducible AmNblA-dependent change in the molecular and/or
spectroscopic state of AmPBS that is distinguishable from untreated
AmPBS and can subsequently be tested as a substrate for AmNblB**.

**Initial reconstruction**

The minimal initial comparison is:

$$
\text{AmPBS} \quad \text{vs.} \quad \text{AmPBS} + \text{AmNblA}
$$

followed, if informative, by concentration- and time-dependent experiments.
### Readouts

**Mass photometry (Refeyn TwoMP).**\
Mass photometry will test whether AmNblA treatment redistributes
sufficiently large AmPBS-derived particle populations. The objective is
not to detect free AmNblA itself but to identify changes in native
antenna assemblies and potential intermediates.

**Fluorescence spectroscopy (Jobin Yvon Fluorolog-3).**\
Steady-state excitation/emission spectroscopy will determine whether
AmNblA-dependent structural changes are accompanied by changes in the
PCB/PBP photophysical state.

**Biochemical separation.**\
Native separation, density gradients, electrophoresis, chromatography,
and/or proteomics will be used as required to determine the molecular
composition of reproducible states.

### Possible outcomes

**Outcome A --- discrete molecular intermediates.**\
Reproducible particle populations emerge after AmNblA treatment. These
become candidate substrates for Aim 4.

**Outcome B --- heterogeneous disassembly.**\
AmNblA produces broad fragmentation without stable intermediates. NblB
experiments will then focus on substrate distributions rather than a
single AmPBS\* species.

**Outcome C --- local remodeling without major mass change.**\
Fluorescence or biochemical changes occur without a large TwoMP shift,
suggesting altered interfaces, pigment coupling, or accessibility rather
than large-scale disassembly.

**Outcome D --- no reproducible effect under validated conditions.**\
The hypothesis that isolated AmNblA generates a stable/measurable AmPBS
intermediate would be weakened, and simultaneous or multi-component
mechanisms would become higher priority.

# Chapter IV --- Structural and biochemical mechanism of AmNblB

## Aim 4. Determine how AmNblB recognizes and remodels AmPBS-derived substrates

### Rationale

NblB is required for normal PBS degradation and has been implicated in
PBP/bilin remodeling [@levi2018]. Its homology to bilin-lyase-related
proteins suggests a molecular connection to chromophore chemistry, but
available biochemical data do not support a simple model in which NblB
acts as an autonomous reverse lyase. NblA and NblB can influence
multiple lyase/PBP reactions, and direct NblA--NblB binding was not
detected in the Hu et al. system [@hu2020].

The key mechanistic question is therefore not simply whether NblB
"removes PCB," but **what substrate state NblB recognizes and what
molecular transformation it promotes**.

## Aim 4A. Test substrate-state dependence of AmNblB activity

The core experimental matrix will distinguish NblA dependence from direct NblA--NblB association:

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{A} & \text{AmPBS} \\
\text{B} & \text{AmPBS} + \text{AmNblA} \\
\text{C} & \text{AmPBS} + \text{AmNblB} \\
\text{D} & \text{AmPBS} + \text{AmNblA} + \text{AmNblB} \\
\text{E} & \text{AmPBS} \xrightarrow{\text{AmNblA pretreatment}} \text{AmPBS}^* + \text{AmNblB}
\end{array}
$$

Exact concentrations, stoichiometries, incubation times, temperatures, and biological/technical replicate numbers are **N/A** pending assay optimization and sample-budget determination.

### Model discrimination

**Model 1 --- sequential substrate remodeling**

$$
\text{AmPBS} \xrightarrow{\text{NblA}} \text{AmPBS}^* \xrightarrow{\text{NblB}} \text{AmPBS}^{**}
$$

**Prediction:** NblA-pretreated AmPBS should respond differently to AmNblB than untreated AmPBS.

**Model 2 --- simultaneous/substrate-mediated cooperation**

Prediction: simultaneous AmNblA + AmNblB produces a
molecular/spectroscopic state not reproduced by NblA pretreatment
followed by NblB.

**Model 3 --- parallel activity**

Prediction: AmNblB effects are largely independent of prior AmNblA
treatment under the purified conditions.

**Model 4 --- missing-component dependence**

Prediction: the three-component system fails to reproduce chromophore
remodeling despite individually active proteins, indicating requirement
for lyases or additional degradation machinery.

Mass photometry and Fluorolog measurements will again provide orthogonal
assembly and photophysical readouts.

## Aim 4B. Determine the structural organization of AmNblB

Structural analysis will be used to generate mechanistic hypotheses, not
as an endpoint by itself.

AmNblB crystallization has shown preliminary success in the Harris
laboratory, but the current structural status is **N/A**. The immediate
objectives are therefore:

-   establish the oligomeric/structural organization of AmNblB;
-   identify candidate PBP-interaction surfaces;
-   determine whether a lyase-like internal cavity is structurally
    conserved;
-   map residues previously implicated in NblB function onto the
    experimental structure;
-   compare AmNblB architecture with structurally characterized bilin
    lyases.

If crystallography does not yield a usable structure, structural
prediction and alternative experimental structural methods will be used
as appropriate. Computational models will be treated as hypotheses
rather than experimental structures [@jumper2021].

## Aim 4C. Test structure-derived functional hypotheses

Candidate recognition/catalytic residues or surfaces will be tested by
targeted mutagenesis.

A mechanistically useful mutant should ideally satisfy two conditions:

1.  retain global folding/oligomeric integrity;
2.  selectively alter substrate interaction or remodeling.

Wild-type and mutant AmNblB will be compared using the substrate-state assays developed in Aims 3–4A.

This provides a causal progression:

$$
\text{structure} \rightarrow \text{candidate molecular determinant} \rightarrow \text{mutation} \rightarrow \text{assembly/spectroscopic phenotype}
$$

rather than relying on docking alone.

## Aim 4D. Determine the fate of the bilin chromophore

Fluorescence changes can report changes in pigment environment,
coupling, quenching, or transfer, but **cannot by themselves prove
covalent chromophore detachment**.

An orthogonal analytical method will therefore be required to
distinguish among:

-   PCB remaining covalently attached to PBP;
-   detached/free PCB;
-   PCB transferred to another protein;
-   altered pigment environment without detachment.

The definitive analytical method is currently **N/A**. Candidate
approaches will be selected according to the chemistry observed and
verified Technion capabilities.

# 6. Integration of the four chapters

The proposal is intentionally composed of two experimental systems rather than a single linear pathway. Their integration occurs at the level of molecular principles.

The dinoflagellate chapters ask:

$$
\text{environmental acclimation} \rightarrow \text{antenna reorganization} \rightarrow \text{modified excitation-energy behavior}
$$

The cyanobacterial chapters ask:

$$
\text{regulated degradation signal} \rightarrow \text{antenna substrate remodeling} \rightarrow \text{pigment/protein disassembly and recycling}
$$

Together, the thesis asks how pigment--protein antennae transition between molecular states while responding to physiological demands.

The common analytical logic is:

1.  define the native molecular state;
2.  apply a biologically meaningful perturbation;
3.  identify reproducible remodeled states;
4.  determine their biochemical/structural basis;
5.  connect molecular remodeling to pigment photophysics or degradation
    function.

This provides coherence without implying evolutionary homology between
acpPC and PBS systems.

# 8. Significance

Antenna remodeling is fundamental to photosynthetic fitness because
photon capture must be balanced against photochemical capacity, nutrient
availability, and photodamage risk. High-resolution structures
increasingly provide static views of photosynthetic complexes, but the
molecular transitions connecting those structures to acclimation and
degradation remain less well resolved.

The proposed work addresses this problem at two scales. In
Symbiodiniaceae, it tests how chronic environmental acclimation is
encoded in the biochemical state of a membrane antenna central to
coral-associated dinoflagellate photosynthesis. In cyanobacteria, it
reconstructs a regulated antenna-degradation module using purified
native and recombinant components and asks how a degradation substrate
is generated and subsequently recognized.

A broader outcome will be an experimentally grounded framework for
thinking about photosynthetic antennae as ensembles of regulated
molecular states rather than fixed structures.

# 10. Risks, limitations, and alternative strategies

## 10.1 acpPC isolation may perturb native organization

Detergent solubilization can alter higher-order antenna organization.
Therefore, increased purity will not automatically be interpreted as
increased biological relevance. Multiple detergent/separation conditions
and less-disrupted membrane fractions will be compared where necessary.

## 10.2 Five strains create excessive combinatorial scope

The five-strain panel is intended for comparative screening. Deep
structural/photophysical work will focus on a limited number of
informative contrasts selected from the screen.

## 10.3 Whole-cell optical phenotypes are not acpPC-specific

Culture absorption, fluorescence, and flow cytometry provide phenotypic
context but cannot establish acpPC molecular mechanisms without
biochemical fractionation.

## 10.4 Mass photometry has a lower molecular-mass limit

TwoMP will primarily be used to study PBS-derived assemblies and
complexes rather than free NblA or small isolated proteins.

## 10.5 Fluorescence does not prove chromophore detachment

Spectral changes will be interpreted as changes in photophysical state
until an orthogonal chemical/biochemical assay establishes chromophore
fate.

## 10.6 NblA/NblB may be insufficient for complete reconstruction

PBS degradation involves additional factors, including bilin lyases,
proteases, and NblD-related pathways
[@hu2020; @krauspe2021; @krauspe2022]. Failure of the minimal purified
system to reproduce complete degradation will be used to identify
missing biochemical requirements rather than interpreted automatically
as failure of the hypothesis.

# 14. Dinoflagellate novelty audit --- revision note

A dedicated literature audit was performed before this revision. It
changes the emphasis of Chapters I--II in four important ways:

1.  **Photoacclimation is established background, not the novelty
    claim.** Irradiance-dependent changes in photosynthetic units,
    pigments, and chlorophyll--protein complexes have been demonstrated
    previously.
2.  **AcpPC architecture is no longer broadly "unknown."** 2024 cryo-EM
    structures provide high-resolution PSI--AcpPCI reference
    architectures.
3.  **Oligomerization is a candidate mechanism, not the presumed
    mechanism.** Published oligomeric acpPC work demonstrates strong
    photophysical consequences but also shows that purification can
    alter assembly state.
4.  **The principal gap is the missing bridge from chronic acclimation
    to native molecular antenna state and from that state to pigment
    energetics/function.**

The strongest current novelty formulation is:

> **Directly connect chronic irradiance acclimation to the biochemical
> state of native acpPC-containing membrane antennae across multiple
> Symbiodiniaceae strains and test whether defined molecular states are
> associated with altered pigment energetics and
> excitation-energy-transfer behavior.**

A final novelty search should be repeated immediately before proposal
submission.
