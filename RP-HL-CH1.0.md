# Chapter 1 — Molecular Organization of Photosynthetic Antennae in Symbiodiniaceae

## Background and rationale

Symbiodiniaceae encompass diverse dinoflagellates with distinct physiological capabilities and associations with animal hosts. Cultured isolates have demonstrated differences in their capacity for photoautotrophic, mixotrophic and heterotrophic growth, as well as their compatibility with a common host (Xiang et al., 2013). 

This biological diversity provides a context for investigating how different members of the family organize their photosynthetic machinery.

At the molecular level, the coexistence of soluble PCP and membrane-associated acpPC provides two distinct routes for organizing peridinin and chlorophyll within antenna complexes. 

Biochemical characterization of these systems has established reference points for protein identity, pigment association and oligomeric organization (Jiang et al., 2012, 2014). 

Structural analysis has further revealed how acpPC proteins assemble around the photosystem I core and organize a pigment network that supports excitation energy transfer (Zhao et al., 2024). 

Together, these studies provide a basis for connecting the properties of isolated antenna complexes to their molecular architecture.

Building on these findings, this chapter asks which antenna assemblies can be recovered from distinct Symbiodiniaceae strains and how their composition and organization relate to their spectroscopic properties. 

Addressing this question requires establishing the identity and assembly state of the recovered complexes alongside their absorption and fluorescence characteristics. 

A central consideration is whether distinct purified fractions represent different antenna populations or changes introduced during isolation. 

Reproducibility across independent preparations and assessment of the effects of purification conditions will therefore be essential for interpreting the recovered assemblies.
