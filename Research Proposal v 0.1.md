# Research Proposal — Working Scientific Framework

## Working Concept

### Core system
Membrane-bound **chlorophyll a–chlorophyll c₂–peridinin protein complexes (acpPC)** from five axenic Symbiodiniaceae strains:

- *Symbiodinium linucheae* — SSA01
- *Symbiodinium necroappetens* — SSA02
- *Symbiodinium pilosum* — SSA03
- *Breviolum minutum* — SSB01
- *Effrenium voratum* — SSE01

The five strains will initially function as a **broad comparative discovery panel**, rather than as five systems requiring equally deep characterization.

---

# Biological Problem

Symbiodiniaceae experience substantial variation in irradiance and possess specialized membrane-bound light-harvesting systems containing chlorophylls, peridinin, and other associated pigments.

Previous work establishes that irradiance acclimation can alter:

- antenna abundance;
- pigment allocation;
- expression/abundance of light-harvesting proteins;
- photoprotective pigment composition;
- overall photosynthetic phenotype.

Separately, biochemical and structural studies indicate that differences in acpPC organization can alter:

- pigment arrangement;
- terminal-emitter energetics;
- excited-state lifetimes;
- excitation-energy-transfer pathways.

However, these two bodies of knowledge have not yet been adequately connected mechanistically.

---

# Research Gap

> Although irradiance-dependent changes in Symbiodiniaceae antenna abundance and pigment allocation are established, it remains unclear whether chronic photoacclimation remodels the biochemical organization and chromophore environment of membrane-bound acpPC, and whether such remodeling directly tunes its spectral and excitation-energy-transfer properties.

The unresolved mechanistic bridge is therefore:

**irradiance acclimation**
↓
**acpPC biochemical remodeling**
↓
**chromophore organization / energetic landscape**
↓
**spectral tuning**
↓
**excitation-energy-transfer behavior**

The precise molecular nature of "biochemical remodeling" is deliberately **not predetermined**.

Candidate mechanisms include:

- pigment occupancy/composition;
- acpPC subunit composition;
- oligomeric state;
- chromophore geometry;
- chromophore–protein interactions;
- higher-order organization;
- association with other photosynthetic complexes.

Which mechanism(s) dominate is currently **N/A**.

---

# Central Research Question

> **How does chronic acclimation to different irradiances remodel the biochemical state of membrane-bound acpPC complexes in Symbiodiniaceae, and how does this remodeling affect spectral tuning and excitation-energy-transfer behavior?**

---

# Central Hypothesis

> **Chronic irradiance acclimation induces reproducible changes in the biochemical organization of acpPC complexes, modifying the energetic environment and organization of their bound chromophores and thereby altering spectral properties and excitation-energy-transfer behavior.**

This hypothesis does **not** assume in advance that acclimation changes acpPC oligomerization, pigment stoichiometry, subunit composition, or chromophore geometry.

Those are competing/possibly complementary mechanistic explanations to be tested.

---

# Experimental Philosophy

The project will use a **funnel design**:

**5 strains**
↓
**controlled irradiance acclimation**
↓
**broad biochemical/spectroscopic screening**
↓
**identify strongest mechanistic contrasts**
↓
**select 2–3 informative systems**
↓
**deep biochemical + structural/spectroscopic characterization**
↓
**mechanistic model of acpPC photoacclimation**

This prevents the project from becoming a full:

`5 strains × 3 irradiances × every possible assay`

factorial experiment.

# Aim 3 — Link acpPC Remodeling to Excitation-Energy Transfer

## Question

> Do acclimation-induced biochemical changes in acpPC alter the energetic behavior of its chromophore network?

This aim will test whether the molecular differences identified in Aim 2 have functional photophysical consequences.

Primary phenotypes of interest:

### Spectral tuning

Changes in:

- absorption features;
- fluorescence emission;
- energetic position of terminal emitters.

### Excitation-energy transfer

# Comparative Logic

The five strains provide biological diversity for discovering alternative acpPC photoacclimation strategies.

However:

> **A difference between individual strains must not automatically be interpreted as a genus-level or evolutionary adaptation.**

Primary comparative unit:

**N/A — strain vs lineage/genus**

Phylogenetic relationships and available biological replication must be considered before making evolutionary claims.

The proposal therefore initially focuses on **biochemical mechanism**, not adaptation.

---

# Primary Thesis Claim

If supported by the experiments, the thesis should ultimately establish:

> **Chronic irradiance acclimation modifies the biochemical organization of membrane-bound acpPC, and these molecular changes tune the energetic behavior of its chromophore network.**

A secondary question will be whether different Symbiodiniaceae strains accomplish this remodeling through conserved or divergent biochemical mechanisms.

---

# What This Proposal Does NOT Yet Claim

The proposal does not currently assume that:

- the five strains have complementary absorption spectra;
- acpPC oligomerization changes with irradiance;
- oligomerization is the dominant tuning mechanism;
- chromophore geometry necessarily changes during acclimation;
- all five strains require detailed structural characterization;
- observed strain differences represent evolutionary adaptation;
- acpPC purification preserves the complete native assembly;
- a synthetic acpPC membrane will increase photochemical output.

These remain hypotheses, possibilities, or future directions.

Before expanding the proposal further, the next task is to convert **Aim 1** from a conceptual aim into a falsifiable experimental aim.

This requires defining:

1. what constitutes successful photoacclimation;
2. what material will actually be isolated;
3. how acpPC will be distinguished from other membrane complexes;
4. what constitutes a significant acpPC phenotype;
5. which result advances a strain/condition into Aim 2;
6. what result would falsify the central hypothesis.
