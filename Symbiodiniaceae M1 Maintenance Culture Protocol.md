# Symbiodiniaceae M1 Maintenance Culture Protocol

## Purpose

Establish one fresh **M1 maintenance culture** from each established **S1 F/2 source culture**.

M1 serves as the primary living backup after the original S1 culture has been branched into maintenance, biomass-production, plating, and archive modules.

# Principle

Each M1 culture is prepared by transferring:

- **20 mL S1 culture**
- into **80 mL fresh 1× F/2**

This produces:

- **100 mL final M1 culture**
- in one **250 mL Erlenmeyer flask**
- with a **1:5 inoculum-to-final-volume ratio**

All five strains use the same transfer ratio.

# Pre-work preparation

1. Confirm that each S1 source culture contains living, pigmented cells.
2. Record for each S1 culture:
   - approximate volume;
   - OD750;
   - pigmentation;
   - clumping or sedimentation;
   - microscopy observations.
3. Prepare fresh 1× F/2 according to [[F2 Medium recipe]].
4. Clean the flasks according to [[Cleaning Glassware]].
5. Sterilize the flasks and closures according to [[Autoclaving Protocol]].
6. Label all five M1 flasks before opening the S1 cultures.
7. Confirm incubator space and flask positions.

---

# Procedure

## 1. Arrange the work

Process one strain at a time:

```text
SSA01 → SSA02 → SSA03 → SSB01 → SSE01
```

Use a new sterile pipette for each strain.

## 2. Inspect and mix the S1 culture

For each strain:

1. Inspect the S1 culture before opening.
2. Record visible pigmentation, sedimentation, clumping, and supernatant appearance.
3. Gently swirl the culture until cells are distributed as evenly as practical.
4. Do not vortex.
5. Avoid aggressive disruption of cell aggregates.
6. Remove a microscopy or OD sample after mixing, when required.

## 3. Prepare the M1 flask

For each strain:

1. Add **80 mL fresh sterile F/2** to the correctly labeled 250 mL flask.
2. Add **20 mL of the corresponding S1 culture**.
3. Mix by gentle swirling.
4. Close with a sterile breathable closure.
5. Confirm that the flask label is readable.
6. Place the flask in the incubator.

## 4. Retain the source culture

1. Return the residual S1 culture to the incubator.
2. Do not discard S1 at this stage.
3. Keep S1 as a temporary emergency backup until:
   - M1 is clearly established;
   - the corresponding S2 culture is recovering;
   - no major transfer failure or contamination is evident.

---

# Incubation conditions

Maintain M1 under the current validated liquid-culture conditions:

- Temperature: **26°C**
- Photoperiod: **12 h light : 12 h dark**
- Light intensity at flask level: approximately **40 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹**
- Final culture volume: **100 mL**
- Vessel: **250 mL Erlenmeyer flask**
- Closure: sterile breathable gauze
- Agitation: none unless later validated

Record the actual light intensity at each flask position with the incubator door closed.

# Acceptance criteria

An M1 culture is accepted as the active maintenance replacement when:

- living cells are visible by microscopy;
- chlorophyll pigmentation is retained;
- no obvious contamination is detected;
- culture appearance is stable or improving;
- biomass, pigmentation, or cell abundance is stable or increasing.

A transient decrease in OD750 immediately after transfer is expected because the S1 inoculum is diluted fivefold.

OD750 should not be used alone to judge M1 success because these cultures:

- form aggregates;
- settle rapidly;
- may contain variable amounts of suspended extracellular material;
- may remain healthy despite low bulk OD.

Interpret OD750 together with:

- microscopy;
- pigmentation;
- visible precipitated biomass;
- chlorophyll autofluorescence, when available.

---

# Failure or weak recovery

If an M1 culture loses pigmentation, shows extensive cell damage, or fails to establish:

1. Re-examine the residual S1 source by microscopy.
2. Confirm that the correct medium and transfer volumes were used.
3. Check incubator light, temperature, and flask position.
4. Check for excessive evaporation.
5. Check for contamination.
6. If S1 remains healthy, prepare a replacement M1 using a fresh S1 inoculum.
7. Consider a larger inoculum only as a documented rescue condition.
8. Consider strain-specific IMK optimization through the master plan if F/2 recovery remains poor.

Do not discard the corresponding S1 source until the replacement M1 has recovered.

---

# M1 retirement and renewal

M1 should function as the active maintenance line, not as a repeatedly topped-up old culture.

When M1 requires renewal:

1. Prepare a fresh maintenance flask.
2. Transfer a defined inoculum from healthy M1 into fresh F/2.
3. Assign the next generation code, such as:

```text
SSA01-M2-DATE
```

4. Retain the previous maintenance generation temporarily until the new culture establishes.
5. Avoid indefinitely refilling the same flask.

# Notes

- M1 is the highest-priority living backup branch.
- All M1 cultures use the same **20 mL into 100 mL** transfer design.
- M1 establishment should precede S1 harvest or retirement.
- M1 is independent from S2, F2P1, A1, and H1, but remains linked to them through the master branching plan.
